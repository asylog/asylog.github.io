<https://ipo.anthropic.com/us/#roadshow-video>

> Achieving Anthropic's mission requires staying at the frontier, and staying at the frontier depends on compute. Our approach here goes back to research that I did with Several of my other co-founders on scaling laws. We found that with the right recipe, more compute reliably and predictably yields more intelligence. Later, we found that it was possible to improve this recipe. over time to achieve more intelligence with the same amount of physical compute. So by the time we started Anthropic in 2021, we knew two things. One, compute is the foundation of the business, and two, getting the most out of every unit of compute is key to making increasingly intelligent models. That's why we're so focused on efficiency, and it's why we believe a dollar of compute at anthropic goes further than a dollar at any other frontier lab. We've achieved that advantage in two ways I'll start with the first, our proprietary approach to bringing up and deploying Silicon. We train and serve Claude now across all four major chip families.
>
> That's only possible because early on we made an unconventional bet on a multi-chip strategy. At the time, many people thought that running AI models on different chips wasn't worth the effort. Each chip needs its own tailored software stack and inference stack, which is expensive and difficult to build. We've now been doing it for years, and we've gotten incredibly good at it. This comes with two key advantages. In a supply-constrained compute market, our multi-silicon strategy allows us to focus on the best price performance regardless of the provider. We can match each chip to the job that it's best at. Some are better at training, some are better at inference. Our multi-silicon chip strategy means we can treat all of our chips, across providers, architectures, and locations as one fungible pool of compute.
>
> This has also made us really good at bringing up new chips. As an example, this year, we started working with AMD GPUs for the first time. In the past, bringing on a new chip platform took dozens of engineers months of work This time, we had Mythos try it out. It got Claude running on AMD in just three days, with basically no human intervention. Claude ported or wrote almost 100% of the kernels. We also have a distinct approach to the data centers that house our chips. Historically, we bought compute as a cloud service. We've now added a second approach where we lease chips and run them in purpose-built data centers designed for us.
>
> We call this anthropic direct. That approach has a couple advantages for us. It helps to lower the total cost of ownership of compute and allows us to separate the commitment to data centers from the chips. The second driver of our compute advantage is our rate of discovery of compute multipliers. We focus on total effective compute, which is the multiplicative product of physical capacity times the compute multipliers that we discover. Each one we discover makes our pool of total effective compute bigger. For example, say you need 100 units of compute to train a model of a certain intelligence. A 4x compute multiplier means you can use 25 units of compute to train the exact same model. Or you can use those hundred physical units to train an even more intelligent model that would have previously required 400 units.
>
> Compute multipliers create a competitive advantage for us that's hard to replicate. They build on each other. Each new multiplier we discover has a positive effect on the multipliers before it. They stack. We believe that this can accelerate even further as we approach recursive self-improvement. Claude will be able to discover compute multipliers entirely by itself, allowing the number of compute multipliers to grow faster and faster. We believe this combination of smart investments, breakthrough research, and the improving capabilities of Claude have put Anthropic in a leading position among the Frontier Labs.
